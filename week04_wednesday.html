Rings and pockets in the car city (Renninger et al., 2025)

  • Isolated rings: suburban bands whose residents mix mostly with each other
  • Segregated pockets: central blocks drawing visitors from one income band
  • Rings sit 10–25 km out, four to ten times the walking city's 2.5 km radius
  • Distance from the centre, then income, then race predict ring or pocket

Cobb County, Georgia, northwest of Atlanta. Idawriter, via Panoramio. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

16

The commuting field expands (Beer, 2025)

  • Central Coast, New South Wales: 30,000 residents in 1945, nearly 300,000 by 2001
  • A third of the region's workforce commuted 50 km or more to Sydney by the 1990s
  • Households bought coastal amenity rather than only cheaper land
  • The commuting field widened faster than the built-up area

Same city, same plazas, different walk (Norwood et al., 2024)

  • 97% of elite compounds sit within a 5-minute walk of a plaza
  • 52% of everyone else's do
  • The plazas are neither sited in elite districts nor closed to anyone, so the gap comes entirely from where people already live
21

Allocating movement

  • Road space is finite
  • Dividing road space is a political act as well as an engineering one
  • The division repeatedly favours through-movement over frontage use
  • Smallest and least organised users usually lose
22

Water and movement share a channel

  • An intramural canal is a street carrying water
  • A street graded for wheels is also a drain
  • Converting the channel for one function converts the channel for both

Bridge over the Beijing–Hangzhou Grand Canal. Wikimedia Commons.

What to take away

  • A travel-time budget capped the pre-industrial city: one hour a day at walking pace (Bakker & Rauch, 2026)
  • Transportation technologies have raised the cap: railway journeys at 10% of coach time
  • Transportation modes affect urban geographies: rings, wedges, many centres
