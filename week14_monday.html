Black political leverage before 1917 (Lumpkins, 2006)

  • John Robinson ran the Illinois State House janitorial staff from 1897
  • James Reese became street commissioner in 1900
  • Mollman won the 1915 mayoralty by 27 votes on Black margins
  • October 1916: at least 200 Black names purged from the rolls

Who was tried (Lumpkins, 2006)

  • 143 white persons indicted on 16 August 1917
  • Brought to trial: 112 white men, 15 Black men, 5 white women
  • Mayor and officials indicted in September, charges dropped
  • No prominent white businessman, manager or politician convicted

16

Black labour and a shrinking industrial base (Lumpkins, 2006)

  • Labourers were 66% of employed Black men in 1920, 78% by 1924
  • The industrial workforce fell from 17,435 men in 1918 to 7,895 in 1921
  • 147 of 398 employed Black women were laundresses in 1920
  • In 1932, 11,000 of 16,000 Black residents sought state relief

Segregation, drafted and recorded

18

Petition and subdivision covenants (Gordon, 2023)

Gordon (2023).

Petition: owners of lots already built and sold sign an agreement adding the same restriction to each of their own deeds. Subdivision: a developer writes the restriction into every deed in a new tract before the first lot is sold.

19

Redlined St. Louis

Gordon (2023). Exact figures: 92.3%, 56.9% and 88.9%.

HOLC grade: the A-to-D rating the federal Home Owners' Loan Corporation put on each neighbourhood of its 1930s security maps, A best and D worst. Redlining: withholding or repricing mortgage credit in the areas graded D, which the maps drew in red.

20

Shelley v. Kraemer, and after (Gordon, 2023)

  • Over 16,000 white homeowners signed restriction petitions
  • Shelley v. Kraemer (1948) began on a petition-restricted block
  • Enforcement did not end with Shelley v. Kraemer
  • The Real Estate Exchange instructed members to carry on

Incorporation as an instrument (Gordon, 2013)

  • Berkeley incorporated in 1937 to avoid schooling with Kinloch
  • Black Jack incorporated in 1970 to block mixed-income housing
  • FHA standards held that neighbourhoods should hold "the same racial and social classes"
  • St. Louis held 750,000 in 1960; Champ, incorporated 1959, held 14
26

Three defeated consolidation votes (Gordon, 2013)

  • 1930: a federal and regional government plan lost statewide
  • 1959: the metropolitan charter lost 2 to 1 in the city
  • 1962: the Borough Plan lost by nearly 4 to 1
  • The 1962 plan proposed 22 boroughs under one regional government

The segregated periphery

30

Clearance and displacement

  • One family in ten displaced by renewal and highways, overwhelmingly African American (Gordon, 2013)
  • Extreme-income neighbourhoods: 15% of families in 1970, over 30% by 2007
  • 31% of Black residents below the poverty line in 1970, the highest share of any major US city (Bourgois, 1989)

Arthur Rothstein, March 1936. Library of Congress, Farm Security Administration collection, titled "Slums. Saint Louis, Missouri".

31

Where the segregation went

Prener (2021), within a 1,526 km² study area (about 120 times the area of Easton, PA).

Index of Concentration at the Extremes: a segregation measure running from −1 to +1, scoring a place by the balance of its most and least advantaged residents. Highly segregated here is −0.333 or below (Prener, 2021).

32

North City after 2000 (Mallach, 2018)

  • 14 of 18 middle-income tracts fell below the threshold by 2015
  • Home sales in the 18 tracts fell from 545 to 95
  • 75% of 2005 purchase mortgages in the 18 tracts were high-cost, against 29% citywide
  • Walnut Park West lost $35m in equity; Shaw gained $47m
33

What to take away

  • Segregation in St. Louis was drafted, signed and recorded: 763 covenants, 30,954 parcels (Gordon, 2023)
  • Metropolitan growth outran a boundary fixed in 1876: 86 municipalities, three defeated consolidations (Gordon, 2013)
  • Segregated space moved from the city to the county and grew about 35 times over (Prener, 2021)
