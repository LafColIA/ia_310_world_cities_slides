How we came to measure it

06

A word that replaced other words (Haffner & Hulse, 2021)

Peter Facey, geograph.org.uk, CC BY-SA 2.0.

  • "Affordability" displaced an older vocabulary
  • The slum problem, the housing shortage, housing need
  • A ratio replaced a description of conditions

07

From survey finding to lending rule

The housing-cost ratio from budget survey to lending rule, 1857-1981 (Haffner & Hulse, 2021).

08

Forty countries, one benchmark

BIS Housing Affordability Index; 100 means a median-income household exactly qualifies (Biljanovska, Fu & Igan, 2023).

  • Advanced economies average 118 against 85 for emerging markets
  • Index values: Hong Kong 29, Turkey 30, Australia 37
  • Eastern Europe tops the ranking

Success generates the problem

10

The more 'triumphant' cities are in expanding productivity through positive agglomeration externalities and the more efficacious their planners are in creating livable urban environments, the more will their demand-induced housing affordability pressures intensify.

Galster & Lee (2020)

11

A national growth bottleneck

  • A model puts three cities' housing constraints at 36% of forgone US growth (Hsieh & Moretti, 2019)
  • New York, San Francisco, San Jose
  • In the same model the median city's constraint level recovers that growth
12

A burden already skewed (Ioannides & Ngai, 2024)

Stacey Harris, geograph.org.uk, CC BY-SA 2.0.

  • OECD bottom quintile spent 37% of income on housing, top quintile 25%
  • Bottom quintile's share rose 9 points since 2005
  • Three times the top quintile's increase

13

Housing as a necessity good

  • A 1% productivity rise predicts a 2% narrower East German gap (Cardullo & Sechi, 2026)
  • Standard preferences predict only 0.4%
  • Amplification depends on housing's budget share
14

Affordability as a competitive lever

  • Doubling affordability predicts about 45% more patent applications (He et al., 2025)
  • The effect runs through population clustering
  • 4.5× larger in high-R&D cities

What is driving it

16

The state stepped back

  • OECD public housing investment fell from 0.17% of GDP to 0.06% (Lee et al., 2022)
  • A 65% cut between 2001 and 2018
  • The UK sold its stock from 1980
17

One driver survives the controls

  • Black households carry higher cost burdens at every point, 1980–2017 (Hess et al., 2020)
  • Covariates fully explain the White burden
  • The Black burden keeps rising after adjustment

Housing as an asset class

19

Bought at scale (Dancygier & Wiedemann, 2024)

King of Hearts, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

  • European institutional acquisition rose from 10% to nearly 30%
  • Investors bought nearly a quarter of US single-family homes in 2021
  • Institutions held 92% of Vonovia by 2023

Financialization: the shift of a good into the hands of investors who hold it for financial return, so that housing is priced and traded as a portfolio asset rather than as shelter.

Per capita, the Nordics lead

Dancygier and Wiedemann (2024), transaction data across 18 OECD countries, 2007 to 2021.

21

For institutional investors, housing is neither shelter nor social right, but simply a tradable asset meant to yield high returns.

Dancygier & Wiedemann (2024)

Does framing move more than rent?

Two message treatments against control, N = 5,087 (Dancygier & Wiedemann, 2024).

23

Berlin voted to expropriate

  • 58% voted to expropriate landlords holding over 3,000 apartments (Dancygier & Wiedemann, 2024)
  • Nearly 400,000 signatures, twice the requirement
  • 83% say the state must ensure housing access
24

The correlation is between capitals (Nijskens et al., 2019)

Colin and Kim Hansen, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

  • Amsterdam rose 55% while the Netherlands rose 21%
  • Greater London 95%, prime central London 140%
  • Seoul apartments 31% against 13% nationally
