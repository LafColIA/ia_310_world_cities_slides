Street navigability and collective rule

Blanton and Fargher (2011), space-syntax measures of eleven pre-modern cities at their focal periods.

07

What space syntax measures (Blanton and Fargher, 2011)

  • Space syntax measures how easily a street network can be moved through
  • Eleven cases: Rome, Beijing, Athens, Tenochtitlan, Edo, Kumase
  • The comparison runs across polities rather than across sizes
  • Edo is the largest case and the least navigable of the eleven

Space syntax: a method that scores a street network by how visible and reachable each point is from the others.

How concentrated the scaling gains are

Arvidsson, Lovsjö and Keuschnigg (2023). Exponents are for six city outcomes scaling with population, from Swedish wage and inter-firm mobility records, US patents and grants, and VKontakte friendship networks.

11

Two mechanisms behind the tail (Arvidsson et al., 2023)

  • Learning and interaction opportunities in large cities are unequally accessed
  • Cumulative advantage: early gains compound across a career
  • Selective migration: tail earners 2.2 times likelier to leave a small city
  • Cumulative advantage and selective migration explain about half the tail difference
12

When accounting for the cost of living in larger cities, many big-city dwellers will in fact be worse off as compared with similar people living in smaller places.

— Arvidsson, Lovsjö and Keuschnigg, 2023

13

Density and mobility in US counties, 1978 to 2015

Connor et al. (2025), counties across the conterminous United States. All three coefficients predict intergenerational mobility, defined as the average adult income percentile of children born to households at the 25th percentile.

14

Poverty, race and the density result (Connor et al., 2025)

  • The density association is significant in high-poverty tracts
  • Significant where the Black population share is high
  • No relationship in low-poverty or low-Black-share tracts
  • Some of the highest upward mobility sits in rural Iowa and Minnesota

What kind of right?

  • Attoh presses Hohfeld's rights typology onto the right to the city (Attoh, 2011)
  • Claim, liberty, power and immunity rights are not interchangeable (Attoh, 2011)
  • The prior question is what conceptual status a right to the city has (Attoh, 2011)
  • The right-to-the-city frame has united analysts more than activists (Uitermark et al., 2012)
