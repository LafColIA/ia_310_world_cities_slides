Three drivers move the gradient

  • Income up: the better-off buy more space and locate farther out
  • Commuting cost down: the gradient flattens and the city sprawls
  • Population up: settlement thickens outward and the fringe expands
09

Is height the missing variable? (Ribeiro et al., 2025)

  • Cities with taller buildings sit below the classical area-population line
    • double the population and built area grows only about 1.5–1.6×
    • area follows population divided by average building height
10

Functional differentiation (Kim et al., 2024)

  • Product complexity sets market radius at the scale of a block
  • High-complexity Seoul clusters correlate with labour population, r = 0.56
  • The same clusters correlate negatively with residential population, r = −0.57
11

High- and low-complexity products (Kim et al., 2024)

  • Convenience stores: complexity 0.26, markets 0.8 km apart
  • Second-hand luxury goods: complexity 0.46, markets 1.6 km apart
  • Diamond traders: complexity 0.84, markets 2.9 km apart

Product complexity: the Product Complexity Index, rescaled 0 to 1, where higher values mark goods carried in fewer and more central places (Kim et al., 2024).

Distance is three times as expensive (Deffebach et al., 2025, working paper)

  • US commuting semielasticity with respect to road distance is about 0.1
    • in poorer countries the average rises to 0.35
    • car access flattens the gradient; slow or informal transit steepens the gradient

Semielasticity: a proportional change in one quantity for a one-unit change in another. Here: the proportional fall in how many people commute for each extra kilometre, measured along the road network rather than in a straight line (Deffebach et al., 2025, working paper).

15

Rings and pockets (Renninger et al., 2025)

  • Isolated rings: suburban bands whose residents mix mostly with each other
    • isolated rings sit 10–25 km out, in city after city
  • Segregated pockets: central blocks drawing visitors from one income band
  • Distance from the centre, then income, then race predict ring or pocket

Do rising rents redraw a cluster? (Cottineau & Arcaute, 2020)

  • "Tech City" branding pulled Google, Cisco and Vodafone into Shoreditch
    • rents followed and the small firms that built the cluster moved out
  • Temple, London's legal cluster, is unchanged at every threshold
    • Temple's tenants were never cheap enough to displace
