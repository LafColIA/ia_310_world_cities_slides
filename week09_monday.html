Are informal settlements a temporary stage?

Makoko, Lagos. Kateregga1, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

  • About 1.1 billion people without legal tenure or basic services (UN-Habitat)
  • 123 articles from 47 countries synthesized (Tenaw et al., 2026)
  • Colonial land practice set the pattern (Tenaw et al., 2026)

09

Four locks, not one (Tenaw et al., 2026)

  • Institutional: weak governance, insecure tenure
  • Economic: no formal credit, precarious work
  • Political: patronage, corruption, displacement
  • Environmental: marginal land, climate vulnerability
10

The solution lies in inclusive, just, and urban policies that integrate informal settlements into the broader city fabric without erasing their identity or community practices.

Tenaw et al. (2026)

Buenos Aires closed the legal pathway, 1977 (van Gelder et al., 2016)

Villa population, City of Buenos Aires, 1976–2010.

  • Land Use Law 8.912 in 1977 ended the loteos populares
  • A sprawl-control measure, in its own terms
  • Infrastructure standards make land ownership inaccessible to poor households

14

The settlement grew a rental market (van Gelder et al., 2016)

A villa miseria, Buenos Aires. Aleposta, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

  • About half of villa residents were renters by 2010
  • Rents in some villas quadrupled between 2005 and 2010
  • Federal District land nearly tripled over the same years

15

While the villa remained the point of entry to the city for poor newcomers, the rented room became their point of entry to the villa.

van Gelder, Cravino & Ostuni (2016)

The price of being formal (Goytia et al., 2023)

Puerto Madero, Buenos Aires. Martin St-Amant, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

  • Formal-sector access premium about 10% of median lower-income household income
  • Mostly tenure security and infrastructure

Public rental housing in 232 Chinese cities (Cao et al., 2024)

Minxin Jiayuan public rental housing, Chongqing, 2011. 杨志强Zhiqiang, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

  • About 70% of 232 cities show supply-demand mismatch
  • Only 31% achieve ideal matching, on 16.12 million units
  • Each 0.1-unit rise in fiscal autonomy tracks 13% less supply

Social ties after rehousing, Casablanca (Ben Haman, 2025)

Alex van Oostenrijk, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0. Xipamanine, Maputo: a stand-in, since the library holds no image of Lebrahma or Al Marouwa.

  • 1,100 residents moved from Lebrahma into four-storey blocks
  • A random plot lottery scattered former neighbours
  • Mutual aid and reciprocity were the community's real infrastructure

There's nothing sociable about all these new apartments. They look like prison cells. It's about keeping people separate and isolated.

Does community outweigh the dwelling? (Kshetrimayum et al., 2020)

What has been tried

22

In-situ upgrading: The Kigali case (Ono & Adrien, 2024)

  • 32 resident-initiated facilities: roads, drainage, bridges, a tap
  • 14 from single households, 10 from neighborhood funds
  • Land regularization covered 70% of Kigali, the enabling condition
23

Five instruments, defined

  • Land readjustment: pooling parcels, servicing them, replotting owners onto smaller serviced lots
  • Inclusionary zoning: a share of new homes required below market price
  • Performance-based codes: rules stating outcomes rather than prescribing built form
  • Low-cost construction systems: cheaper industrialised building methods, manufactured locally
  • Public housing provision: dwellings built, owned or subsidised by the state
24

Land readjustment, in principle

Diagram drawn for this course; mechanism after García Fermín (2024).

Land readjustment: pooling adjacent parcels, taking a share of the pool for streets and a further share to sell, then replotting each owner onto a smaller parcel that is serviced.

25

Land readjustment in practice (García Fermín, 2024)

  • Medellín's PILaR pilot: stalled at design in 2015, never implemented
  • Medellín's Juan Bobo: relabelled readjustment, 78% municipal land, no pooling
  • Ethiopia's Lidata: 1,093 of 1,100 informal families displaced
26

Inclusionary zoning by set-aside share (Azlan et al., 2025)

Share of studies reporting positive integration, by set-aside band; 50-study review.

Inclusionary zoning: a rule requiring a share of the homes in a new development to be sold or let below market price, usually in exchange for extra density or a fee.

27

Performance-based codes in Queensland (Gallagher et al., 2025)

  • Queensland went performance-based in 1997
  • Developers still conform to prescriptive codes for approval certainty
  • Site cost ranked first for 40% of 231 projects
28

Low-cost construction systems (Muhammed et al., 2025)

  • Materials and labour are 60–80% of housing cost
  • Reusable plastic formwork cuts material and labour costs by up to 35%
  • Cheaper building systems do not touch land, tenure or zoning
29

What to take away

  • Informality tracks policy, not only poverty: a closed legal pathway in Buenos Aires (van Gelder et al., 2016)
  • Settlements may lock in through four reinforcing domains: single-axis fixes fail (Tenaw et al., 2026)
  • Locally based upgrading works under the right conditions (Ono & Adrien, 2024): community environment outweighs the dwelling (Kshetrimayum et al., 2020)
