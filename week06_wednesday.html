Specialisation and food

  • Cheap transport lets a place stop feeding itself (Jedwab, Johnson & Koyama, 2020)
  • That is what specialisation actually means
  • Before cheap transport, local food supply may have capped city size (Bakker & Rauch, 2026)

Railways mattered most where water could not

  • United States, 1890: removing the railways costs 2.7% to 5.5% of GDP (Duranton & Turner, 2026)
  • India, 1860 to 1912: railways account for 62.7% of all per-capita income growth (Bogart et al., 2024)
  • Sub-Saharan Africa: rail potentially accounts for about 55% of the change in urbanization outside the largest cities (Jedwab & Moradi, 2016)
10

Rail made the Midwest into Chicago's hinterland

  • Chicago and the Great West, 19th century: rail made city and countryside one system (Cronon, 1991)
  • Grain elevators and grading turned a farmer's wheat into a fungible commodity
  • Grain, lumber and meat each became a chain running through Chicago

14

The box beat the trade agreements

  • 22 OECD countries, 20 years after adoption: containerization is associated with 790% more bilateral trade (Bernhofen et al., 2013)
  • Same period and method: GATT membership gives 285% and a free-trade agreement 45%
  • Ports without rail container facilities capture 292%, about a third of the full effect
15

The change was in the through-movement, not at the quay

Container stacks, Rotterdam. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

Containerization: moving freight in standard steel boxes that transfer between ship, rail and truck without being opened, so a cargo is handled once rather than piece by piece.

16

A port can become a world city and stay one

  • Jakarta, 17th century to today: its world-city role was built on the VOC entrepôt (Indraprahasta & Derudder, 2019)
  • The authors trace four phases, each inheriting the last
  • What persists is a position in a network rather than a quay
